## What it is

`scripts/laya_trade_set.py` turns closed desk rows into a Laya decision set. The question is whether a trade closed as a winner or a loser, using only facts known at entry.

Laya (`laya-finetune` 0.1.2) is installed in its own environment. It is not on the live scoring path.

## Label

Winner means net above zero. Loser means net below zero. A missing net falls back to usd. Flat closes are counted and left out of the graded files. On 1 Oct 2026 the closed book had 22,455 rows, 2 duplicate identities dropped, and 0 flats, leaving 22,453 graded trades.

## What the card contains

Book, engine, symbol, side, score band, family, regime, reward-to-risk, and stop distance in basis points. Prices are ratios. Net, usd, outcome, exit price, exit time, MAE, MFE, and exit reason stay off the card.

Grade A, Grade B, and Expandables can pick up trigger-time score, family, and regime from the triggered-counterfactual plans. That join covered score on 3,622 graded rows and regime on 3,611. Reward-to-risk was present on 22,220.

## Holdout

Sorted by exit time. The earliest 15,717 train. The last 6,736 are the test, and every test close is later than every train close. Synthetic label generation is not used.

On that later slice:

| Model | Accuracy | Brier |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Majority (always loser; train win rate 43.5%) | 59.6% | 0.242 |
| Logistic regression on the same entry fields | 61.2% | 0.238 |
| Laya | not scored | not scored |

The logistic model beats the majority class on both accuracy and Brier. The edge is small. Laya was not trained: this run had no NVIDIA GPU, and the trainer requires one. No Laya accuracy is invented. It does not count as having learned the book.

When a GPU is available:

```
layaft train task=tasks/laya_desk_trades.yaml profile=test teacher=none
layaft val task=tasks/laya_desk_trades.yaml
```

Laya counts only if that validation beats both the majority class and the logistic model on a later slice it did not train on.
